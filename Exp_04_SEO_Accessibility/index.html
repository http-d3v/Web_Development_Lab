🎓 Lab Viva Voce Preparation & Theoretical Concepts

Q1: What are HTML5 Semantic Landmarks and why are they critical for accessibility?

Semantic landmarks (such as <header>, <nav>, <main>, <article>, <section>, <aside>, and <footer>) designate structural regions of a web page. Screen readers provide keyboard shortcuts allowing blind users to jump directly between landmarks without tabbing through every link sequentially.

Q2: What is the Heading Hierarchy rule and why should headings never be skipped?

Heading levels (<h1> through <h6>) define the outline of a document. A page must have exactly one <h1> representing the primary topic, followed sequentially by <h2> for major sections and <h3> for sub-sections. Skipping levels (e.g. going from <h1> to <h4>) creates confusion for assistive screen reader users who navigate via heading indexes.

Q3: What are the minimum color contrast ratios mandated by WCAG 2.1 AA?

WCAG 2.1 Level AA requires a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 for normal text (< 18pt or 14pt bold) and at least 3:1 for large text (≥ 18pt or 14pt bold) and graphical UI components. Level AAA requires an enhanced ratio of 7:1 for normal text.

Q4: Why is <a href="#main" class="skip-link"> essential for motor-impaired and keyboard users?

Users who rely on keyboards or switch devices must press Tab repeatedly to move through links. A skip link placed at the very top of the DOM allows keyboard users to bypass extensive navigation menus and jump directly to the primary page content with a single keystroke.
